Some credit is never paid: the customer moved away, or cannot be reached for months. A write-off stops
chasing it, without pretending the money came.

## 1. Write it off

1. Open the bill under **Orders → Due**.
2. Press **Write off**.
3. In **Why**, write the reason: *Moved away, not reachable for months*.
4. Press **Write off ₹2,700**.
5. A cashier sees **Manager approval needed**, with their reason already filled in — the manager types
   their PIN.

## 2. What changes

- The order leaves **Due** and says **Written off ₹2,700**. The Orders list says *₹2,700 written off*,
  never *Paid*.
- The bill says **BALANCE WRITTEN OFF**, with a *Written off* line.
- No money is counted: the drawer, today's takings and Inventory's Day Book do not change.
- Reports show a **Written off as never paid** line, and **Activity** records who and why.
- Inventory is told, and stops showing it as due.

:::warning The bill does not change
The GST invoice stays exactly as issued: the goods went, the tax is owed either way.
:::

## 3. If they pay after all

Open the order and press **They paid after all**. Take what they pay, up to what was written off. That much
of the write-off goes away; all of it, and the bill says **CREDIT PAID IN FULL**.

## Common problems

:::faq "The goods are still in the shop"
Only what went home unpaid can be written off. A kept order still waiting is cancelled with a return.
:::

:::faq "It cannot be returned or exchanged"
A written-off bill takes no return: the store credit a return would give back was never paid for. A
manager sorts it out with the customer.
:::
